# PhysicsLab visual direction

This file supplies craft criteria for a selected visual review. Product identity
comes from `docs/current/`, especially `DESIGN_DIRECTION.md` and
`MIO_CHARACTER.md`. Use only criteria relevant to the review question; this
reference does not require a new aesthetic or a broader verification packet.

## The real scene

A Belarusian student aged roughly 14-18 opens the site after school, during homework, before a lesson, or while preparing for CT/CE. They may be tired or anxious about physics. The site should feel companionable, intelligent, and visually generous without behaving like a game for small children or a strict electronic textbook.

PhysicsLab is not a space station, a mission control interface, a laboratory simulator, or a SaaS dashboard. Physics is the subject, not an excuse to cover every screen with stars, grids, glass, instruments, and neon.

## The recognizable world

- Preserve the authored illustrated world; light and dark themes are both first-class surfaces.
- Mio is the approved companion; her identity and role follow `docs/current/MIO_CHARACTER.md`.
- Cats or other supporting characters are not required identity elements. Use them only when explicitly approved with a concrete role. Legacy Nova artwork does not establish current product direction.
- Character and scene art is generated raster production art with transparent variants when a character must sit inside the interface. Do not replace it with handcrafted SVG scenes, CSS drawings, emoji, or generic stock illustrations.
- Backgrounds may carry atmosphere, but they need a physical or narrative reason: a desk after school, a classroom object, a window, a notebook, a trolleybus, a light experiment. Generic cosmic fog and particles are not identity by themselves.

## Composition

- Prefer one clear scene or focal relationship per viewport.
- Use asymmetry, overlap, cropping, and negative space intentionally. Empty space is allowed to remain empty.
- The image and the interface should compose together. A character must look toward, point to, hold, illuminate, or otherwise relate to the important content.
- Avoid a page assembled from equal rounded cards. Repetition is allowed only when it matches repeated student behavior, such as a list of tasks or formulas.
- Alternate quiet and dense passages. Learning pages need breathing room around explanations and diagrams. Exam pages may be denser, but should never feel like a control panel.
- On mobile, recompose the scene rather than merely shrink it. Protect faces, hands, relevant objects, formulas, and useful negative space.

## Typography and mathematics

- UI typography should feel contemporary and friendly to a teenager, not corporate, toy-like, or editorial for its own sake.
- Mathematical notation uses KaTeX metrics and a real math font. Prose, variables, units, indices, fractions, and diagram labels must coexist without looking pasted from different products.
- A formula surface belongs to the page palette. A white worksheet rectangle on the dark site is allowed only when the whole composition deliberately establishes a physical sheet; it must not appear as an accidental theme break.
- Headings should sound like useful destinations, not like labels in a design presentation. Avoid tiny decorative metadata, numbered scaffolding, and type used merely to fill a composition.

## Learning and exam preparation

The distinction must be felt before it is explained.

**Learning** can be slower and more spacious. An observable situation, diagram or graph can connect a formula to what changes in the scene. Name quantities where they first matter. Mio may guide attention or embody a misconception when that action serves the lesson; no single sequence is mandatory for every topic.

**CT/CE preparation** is focused and compact. The task, given data, answer, and explanation have a stable rhythm. Navigation and progress help orientation without theatrical countdowns, fake statistics, stress language, or gamified pressure.

Do not separate the modes with paragraphs about the teaching method. The difference should be visible in composition, density, interaction, imagery, and tempo.

## Language on the surface

- Write natural Russian suitable for a Belarusian secondary-school student and use Belarusian school terminology for CT/CE, formulas, units, tasks, and topic names.
- Explain an unfamiliar symbol, projection, sign, or index where it first matters.
- Avoid institutional lecturing and the repeated reassuring phrases of AI tutoring copy.
- Do not write about how the interface teaches, what the student is about to predict, or what "step" the student is on unless a genuine ordered procedure requires it.
- Avoid audit language, implementation language, motivational filler, fake precision, and promises that the material is easy.

## Material and detail

- Surfaces share one material family. Borders, glow, blur, and shadows are not decoration to apply everywhere.
- Small details may come from physics and school life: pencil marks, a folded page corner, a ruler, graph paper, a bus ticket, a prism reflection. Use only when they belong to the scene.
- Motion may reveal a physical relationship or acknowledge an action. Constant floating, pulsing, orbiting, and particle motion makes the site tiring and generic.

## Cultural credibility

- Prefer plausible Belarusian classrooms, homes, clothing, stationery, public transport, weather, and school routines over vague American campus imagery or fantasy laboratories.
- Avoid invented official symbols, inaccurate exam paperwork, and stereotyped national decoration.
- A teacher should be able to show the page in class without needing to excuse the terminology, diagram, or tone.

## What must not creep back in

- station, mission, forecast, cockpit, laboratory-dashboard, or quest framing;
- equal card grids as the default page grammar;
- white formula panels that break the palette without a physical-paper concept;
- characters stranded in corners without a role;
- inconsistent generated character styles and lighting;
- generic purple space gradients, glass panels, glow, decorative particles, and over-rounding;
- paragraphs that announce the vibe or methodology instead of letting the page demonstrate it;
- repeated "step 1 / step 2 / step 3" labels and generic tutor reassurance;
- handcrafted SVG illustrations standing in for finished artwork.
