# Web design decision rules

Status: `CANON`.

Scope: decisions for `apps/web`; this is not a visual-style description.

## Rules

1. Give each visible element an intentional functional, learning, navigational,
   compositional, or experiential role. Decoration is allowed, but thematic
   association alone is not a sufficient role.
2. Make the current action, answer feedback, and next available action more
   prominent than decoration.
3. Use visual differences to distinguish product modes or states only when the
   difference helps a learner act or orient.
4. Keep formulas, diagrams, and graphs semantically tied to the physical claim
   they support. Decorative art does not substitute for a diagram or evidence.
5. Preserve readable quiet space around conditions, answers, and explanations.

## Anti-fixation guardrails

- Do not turn one successful page pattern, illustration, character, colour, or
  teaching interaction into a universal template.
- Do not add literal physics iconography, generated decoration, or a character
  merely to signal that the page is about physics.
- Do not expose internal methodology, route labels, or decorative `01/02/03`
  sequences to learners unless they communicate real progress or state.
- Do not replace approved art or alter a stable composition without a visible
  defect, a changed product need, or new user evidence.
- Do not let optional design-sync previews define product direction.
